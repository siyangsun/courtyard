---
title: Understanding how artists work is necessary to making better creative AI products
---
last updated in 2023/2024; i felt like these ideas were prescient and rarely spoken of at the time

these thoughts are as easily applicable to LLMs as they are to diffusion models. i just chose to talk about the world of visual communication because i know it better.

artists and technologists got off on the wrong foot. a lack of understanding and some bad apples on both sides

art people are being a bit close-minded about AI. i don't blame them. they've been hurt, insulted, stolen from, trivialized, marginalized, their beloved communities burned down for a quick buck. tech people are also being quite close-minded - not about the AI, but rather about how humans have already been working within this craft for years. it seems like nobody is bothering to ask human creators about their existing workflow - when one of the most basic principles of product is to learn about how your end user interacts with the space, it begs the question on who the end user of AI really is. though everyone is claiming that it's to "augment" existing artists, i have yet to see any tools built (ai upscaling is maybe the only one; generative fill could be useful but it's still a bit too wild as is) that feels like it was designed to augment existing human artists. where are the tools that let me check if my work is in perspective, or check my anatomy, or rotate a small area of the canvas across an axis, or explore multiple lighting angles and color gradients for a thumbnail, or generate a moodboard from text, or generate thumbnails with a moodboard? you can probably do a few of these things with midjourney, some screenshot magic, and a dream - but it's safe to say that is hackery trying to get us far away from where the tools are today. i'm sure you know that even in its current state, a diffusion model can generate some pretty amazing stuff with just a short prompt, if you're lucky. but it usually doesn't. and you can't really control what comes out. everyone is so focused on prompts because that's the only thing they have control over. over time the prompts get more and more sophisticated, from one-shot to few-shots to a-ton-of-shots. this is one way to get more curated results; but the people who have the vocabulary to reliably describe what they want (and a clear vision of what they want), as well as the taste to know what's good, usually are not people who want anything to do with AI. i'm of the opinion that if the AI is generating exactly what you imagined in your head, you either don't have a very developed visual imagination or you should be hired immediately as an art director at disney or riot or blizzard! did you imagine this camera angle, this perspective, this depth of field, this color composition, this ratio of lights to darks that emphasize the drama and guide your eyes? i'm not even talking about the subject or environment or style of brushwork here. did you think about these things? or did you just have a rough idea in your head of some type of "vibe" and the AI offered you something closer to that and more lucid than you could have imagined, and so you latched onto that?

it's a classic situation that happens to scenes - somebody powerful stumbles into your community of common interests without caring to understand what makes that community so special in the first place. they invite their poser friends who use the lingo all wrong. they extract whatever social or monetary value they can out of appearing to be a part of this community - then, when they're satisfied, they leave. usually by that point they've made everyone in the community uncomfortable and drawn a lot of unwanted attention from the outside. that being said, artists don't want to gatekeep art! they are some of the most generous people out there, providing free courses and tutorials and coaching. artists love showing you how they work, if you're only so inclined to watch. come in the gate! join us in learning the craft of how to understand and interact with the world with your eyes and hands! but please, come in through the front gate that has literally been made to welcome you, instead of trying to knock down the whole house to get in. you don't even have to pick up the paintbrush yourself, but just ask somebody and try to understand them. i think everybody would be happier this way, and you might just make a killing off some apps that these communities are itching to use and pay for.
